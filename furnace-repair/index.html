A modern furnace runs a strict startup sequence, and knowing where the sequence breaks is most of the diagnosis. The thermostat calls, the inducer motor spins up to prove draft through a pressure switch, the hot surface igniter glows, the gas valve opens, the burners light, the flame sensor confirms fire, and only then does the blower deliver heat to the house. In Oshkosh, WI, the failures cluster at predictable points: a flame sensor coated in oxide that can no longer prove the flame, a hot surface igniter cracked from years of thermal cycling, a pressure switch that will not close because the inducer is weak or the condensate trap on a high-efficiency model has clogged, or a control board with a burned relay. Each one stops the sequence cold, and each one mimics the others from the hallway.

Oshkosh HVAC repairs furnaces by testing the sequence, not guessing at it. Our experienced technicians verify each stage with a meter rather than a hunch: igniter resistance, flame signal in microamps, pressure switch operation against actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor health. Every visit includes the safety fundamentals on a fuel-burning appliance: the heat exchanger inspected, connections leak-tested, and draft confirmed, because the difference between a furnace problem and a furnace hazard is not visible from the thermostat. We measure temperature rise against the data plate before we leave so the fire and the airflow are matched the way the manufacturer intended.

Oshkosh HVAC services natural gas, propane, and electric furnaces of every major brand, from standard 80 percent units to condensing high-efficiency models with their traps, drain lines, and dual pressure switches. Our trucks carry the parts that actually fail, such as igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors, so a typical no-heat call ends with the house warming up. Signs You Need Furnace Repair in Oshkosh

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Keep an eye out for the following clues that it's time to bring in an expert:

  • The blower runs constantly but the air never warms
  • Repeated shutdowns just seconds after the burners light
  • The furnace clicks and glows but the burners never light
  • Booming or banging at light-off from delayed ignition
  • A flame that burns soft and yellow rather than steady blue
  • A furnace that shuts off every few minutes before the house warms up
  • Squealing or grinding from the blower compartment
  • Rust streaks, soot, or water around the cabinet or flue

Why Hire a Professional for Furnace Repair?

Gas, open flame, high voltage, and a flue share one cabinet in a furnace, and the most dangerous failure is the one you cannot see: a damaged heat exchanger that lets combustion gases into the air the blower circulates. Every morning that delayed ignition keeps booming at startup, that exchanger takes more stress. The diagnostic side has its own pitfalls. When a furnace trips its high limit, the switch itself may be bad, or the furnace may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a failing blower. Swapping the switch in that second case removes a safety device and leaves the actual problem in place.

At Oshkosh HVAC, no symptom gets treated in isolation. A meter tells us the flame signal, so we never condemn a good sensor while the real culprit, a cracked igniter or a weak gas valve, stays in place. We confirm draft at both the pressure switch and the flue, measure temperature rise against the data plate to flag airflow problems early, and test the gas side with a leak detector, not a guess. When we are done, the furnace has proven itself safe and correct through a full cycle, and if it is nearing the end of its useful life, you will get an honest assessment.

Furnace Repair Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Change the filter monthly during heating season; it is the cheapest furnace part you will ever buy
  • Store laundry supplies, especially chlorine products, well away from the furnace closet
  • Book a fall tune-up so weak igniters and fouled sensors are caught before the first freeze
  • Keep the condensate trap and drain clear on high-efficiency units to prevent lockouts
  • Listen at light-off; clean ignition is quiet, and booming means the burners need service
  • Avoid packing the utility room, because burners need open space for combustion air
  • Keep a working carbon monoxide alarm on every level of the home and check it each month
