Think of a heat pump as an air conditioner that can run in reverse. In summer it pulls heat out of your home; in winter a reversing valve flips the refrigerant flow, and the same compressor draws heat from the outdoor air and brings it inside. Because it moves heat instead of creating it, a heat pump can deliver two to three times more heat energy than the electricity it uses, which is a big reason homeowners in Oshkosh, WI are making the switch as older systems wear out. Today's cold-climate models, built around variable-speed inverter compressors, keep producing useful heat well below freezing, far beyond the heat pumps of twenty years ago.

Getting that performance takes design work up front, and this is where Oshkosh HVAC earns the job. Sizing a heat pump means reconciling two loads: the summer cooling demand and the winter heating demand, and finding the balance point where outdoor temperature meets the unit's capacity curve. Our experienced technicians run the load calculation, weigh the compressor staging options against your climate and ducts, and design the backup heat correctly, whether that is electric strips staged to assist below the balance point or a dual-fuel pairing with a gas furnace that takes over in the deepest cold. We also verify the electrical service and the duct system, because heat pumps deliver their heat at lower supply temperatures than furnaces and need real airflow to feel right.

On installation day we follow the same checklist used on every Oshkosh HVAC system. The line set is pressure-tested and evacuated to a micron-verified vacuum, the charge is weighed in and checked with superheat and subcooling, and the outdoor unit goes on a stand wherever winter defrost drainage requires it. Condensate is handled properly, and the thermostat is configured so staging, backup lockouts, and defrost all work as designed. Maintenance Tips for Heat Pump Installation

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Change filters on schedule; low airflow hurts a heat pump in both seasons
  • Plan two tune-ups a year so the system is verified in both heating and cooling
  • Give the outdoor unit open space all year, free of drifts and overgrowth
  • Clean the outdoor coil each spring for a strong start to summer
  • Let the system run steady; big setbacks trigger expensive backup heat on recovery
  • Expect light frost in winter and a periodic defrost hiss; solid ice means call us
  • Keep an eye on winter bills; a sharp increase usually means the backup heat is running too much
