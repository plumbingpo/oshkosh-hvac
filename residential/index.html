Clear Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Nobody likes surprises on the final bill. That's why Oshkosh HVAC prices every Oshkosh job before the work begins, after a full diagnostic that looks at the equipment's age, fuel type, and tonnage, refrigerant levels, coil and heat exchanger condition, and the ductwork feeding each room. You'll know what's wrong, what the fix involves, and what it costs before you say yes.

Many HVAC problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Refrigerant seeps out through pinhole leaks in the evaporator coil, blower bearings wear quietly, and a weakening capacitor gives no warning until the compressor won't start on the first hot day. If you notice weak airflow, rising energy bills, ice on the refrigerant lines, or rooms that never reach the thermostat setting, schedule an inspection right away. Catching it early often keeps a repairable issue from becoming a replacement.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most air conditioners and furnaces last 12 to 18 years, and a planned replacement is always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency swap during a heat wave or cold snap. Our technicians know the climate demands common to Oshkosh and how they affect equipment life, so we can tell you honestly whether your system has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Tune-Ups That Keep Your System Running

A Oshkosh HVAC maintenance visit is far more than a quick filter swap. The technician cleans the coils, verifies the refrigerant charge, tests capacitors and contactors, inspects the burners and heat exchanger, clears the condensate drain, puts in a fresh filter, and checks static pressure in the duct system. Small problems get caught while they're inexpensive to correct, and your system stays running clean and efficient.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with spring cooling tune-ups and fall heating tune-ups for most systems. That's especially important in dusty areas, where dirty condenser coils choke heat rejection and drive up energy use. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your equipment's age, your household's comfort needs, and your local climate.

Consider maintenance as a safeguard for equipment you've already paid for. Each tune-up helps keep the coils clean, the charge correct, and the filter fresh, which lowers monthly energy bills while easing the strain on the compressor and blower. For most families, the plan costs far less than an early replacement.

Sized for Your Home

The Right Size System for Your Home

When it comes to HVAC, bigger is not better. An oversized unit turns on and off too often, burns extra energy, and never runs long enough to pull humidity out of the air, while an undersized one runs nonstop and still falls behind on the hottest days. We don't simply copy the tonnage of the old unit. We run a proper load calculation that factors in your square footage, insulation, windows, and ductwork, and for mini-splits we size each head to the room it serves.

Houses change over time. A finished basement, an addition, or two new bedrooms can outgrow the single-stage system that used to be plenty. Today's two-stage and variable-speed equipment holds tighter temperatures and uses much less energy. If you're torn between a heat pump and a furnace-and-AC pair, or ducted and ductless, we'll lay out the real trade-offs in SEER2 ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting needs, and installation work.

Frequent Failures

The HVAC Repairs Oshkosh Homes Need Most

No cooling, a furnace that refuses to light, ice on the indoor coil, a slow refrigerant leak: every one of these problems has a different cause and calls for a different repair. Oshkosh HVAC diagnoses the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that short-cycles, a thermostat that loses its settings, a clogged condensate drain, or a mini-split throwing error codes.

Not every HVAC problem is equally urgent. A weak capacitor only keeps the compressor from starting. A cracked heat exchanger, on the other hand, can let combustion gases into the house, and a blocked condensate drain can quietly ruin a ceiling before anyone notices. We come prepared to replace capacitors, contactors, fan motors, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards, seal ductwork that's leaking air into the attic, and recharge refrigerant once a leak is fixed.
